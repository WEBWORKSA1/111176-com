Singles’ Day (11.11): From Student Joke to the World’s Biggest Sale

By the 111176 editorial team · Updated 2026-09-29 · 7 min read

Quick answer: 11 November (11.11) looks like four “bare sticks” (光棍), Chinese slang for single people. It began as a student anti-Valentine’s Day in the 1990s. Alibaba turned it into a sale in 2009. By 2025, industry-wide Double 11 GMV was estimated at ¥1,695 billion (≈US$238B) by Syntun.

Origin: 1993, Nanjing

The widely told story traces Singles’ Day to students at Nanjing University in 1993, who picked the date because 11/11 is all ones: four lonely people. It spread through campuses as a day to celebrate (or commiserate) being single.

From joke to shopping festival

In 2009 Alibaba’s Taobao Mall (now Tmall) ran a Singles’ Day promotion. First-year sales were reportedly around US$10 million. The event grew every year, and rivals like JD.com joined. By 2021 Alibaba reported ¥540.3B and JD ¥349.1B in GMV for the period, about US$139B combined (CNBC). Since 2022 neither company publishes a headline figure.

What it means for brands

  • Start early: pre-sales often open in October.
  • Use number psychology: prices ending in 8, bundles of 2 (好事成双), and codes like 1111 or 168.
  • Go beyond China: Singles’ Day promotions now run in Southeast Asia and parts of Europe.
  • Plan the whole calendar: 520 (May 20), 618 (June 18), 11.11 and 12.12.

FAQ

Is Singles’ Day only for single people?

No. Today it is mainly a shopping festival for everyone.

When does 11.11 start?

The official date is 11 November, but major platforms start pre-sales and promotions in mid-to-late October.
