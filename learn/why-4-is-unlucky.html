Why 4 Is Avoided in China (Tetraphobia Explained)

By the 111176 editorial team · Updated 2026-09-29 · 5 min read

Quick answer: 4 is unlucky because 四 (sì) sounds almost the same as 死 (sǐ), “death”. The two differ only in tone. Fear of the number 4 is called tetraphobia.

One tone away from “death”

Mandarin uses tones to tell apart syllables that are otherwise identical. 四 (sì, fourth tone) and 死 (sǐ, third tone) share the same consonant and vowel. In fast speech, and in many regional accents, they are practically the same. The association is strong across the Chinese-speaking world. It also exists in Japanese and Korean, where the reading of 4 (shi, sa) matches the word for death.

Where you’ll see 4 disappear

  • Buildings: many towers in China, Hong Kong, Taiwan and Singapore skip floors 4, 14, 24 and sometimes all floors from 40 to 49.
  • Hospitals and hotels often avoid room numbers with 4.
  • Phone numbers and plates containing 4s sell at a discount or are left unassigned.
  • Gifts: sets of four items are avoided.
  • Domain names: Chinese domain investors treat “no 4” (无4) as a key quality marker for numeric domains.

Combinations to watch

14 (要死, “want to die”), 44 (double death), 74 (气死, “angry to death”), 514 (我要死) and 748 (去死吧, “go die”). Check any price or SKU code in the decoder before you publish it.

When 4 is fine

Context matters. 4 inside a date, a technical spec or a sports score rarely offends anyone. In some slang 4 even stands in for 是 (shì, “yes / is”), as in 4242 (是啊是啊, “yes, yes”) and 9494 (就是就是, “exactly”). Numbers like 1314 (“forever”) contain a 4 and are loved. Meaning comes from the full phrase, not the single digit.

FAQ

Should Western businesses avoid 4?

If you sell to Chinese-speaking customers, avoid 4 in prices, bundle sizes, room numbers and hotlines. It costs nothing and removes a negative signal.

Is 13 unlucky in China?

Not traditionally. 13 is a Western superstition, though it is widely known in China today.
