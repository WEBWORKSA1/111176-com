Why 8 Is the Luckiest Number in Chinese Culture

By the 111176 editorial team · Updated 2026-09-29 · 6 min read

Quick answer: 8 is lucky because 八 (bā) sounds like 发 (fā), as in 发财 (fā cái), “to get rich”. The effect is strongest in Cantonese-speaking southern China and Hong Kong, where the two words sound even closer.

The sound behind the luck

Chinese has many homophones: words that sound the same or nearly the same. Over centuries, many numbers picked up the meaning of the word they sound like. The number eight, 八 (bā), sounds close to 发 (fā), which means to prosper, to develop or to come into wealth. The everyday New Year greeting 恭喜发财 (gōng xǐ fā cái) — “wishing you prosperity” — uses that same 发.

In Cantonese the match is even closer: eight is baat and prosper is faat. That is one reason the obsession with 8 grew especially strong in Guangdong and Hong Kong before it spread nationwide.

8 in the real world

  • The Beijing Olympics opened on 08/08/2008 at 8:08:08 pm local time. Organisers chose the date on purpose.
  • Phone numbers: in August 2020 a Chinese mobile number ending in five 8s sold at a court-run auction for about ¥2.25 million (≈US$324,000), according to reporting by VICE.
  • Licence plates: in Hong Kong, plates with 8s regularly fetch millions of Hong Kong dollars at government auctions.
  • Prices and addresses: retail prices ending in 8 or 88, and apartments on 8th, 18th, 28th or 88th floors, are marketed as premium.
  • Red envelopes: gift amounts such as 88, 168, 888 or 8,888 are common at weddings and New Year.

Using 8 well (and not overdoing it)

For marketers selling to Chinese-speaking customers, ending prices in 8 is a cheap, low-risk signal of good will. But numbers work in combination: 8 next to a 4 (e.g. 84, 48) can be read negatively, and a string of 8s on a cheap product can look gimmicky. FAQ

Is 8 lucky in all of Asia?

8 is strongly lucky in Chinese-speaking regions. Japan also sees 八 as auspicious, partly for its widening shape (末広がり). Other cultures vary.

Is 88 always positive?

Usually. In Chinese chat it also means “bye-bye”. Note that in some Western contexts 88 has extremist connotations, so international brands should check local sensitivities.
