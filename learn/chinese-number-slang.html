Chinese Number Slang: 520, 1314, 666, 88 and More

By the 111176 editorial team · Updated 2026-09-29 · 7 min read

Quick answer: Chinese number slang replaces words with digits that sound similar: 520 (wǔ èr líng) ≈ 我爱你 (wǒ ài nǐ), “I love you”. It started in the pager and early-internet era and is still everywhere on WeChat, Weibo and Douyin.

How number slang works

Each digit has one or two common readings (1 is also “yāo”, 0 is “líng”), and Mandarin has a small set of syllables. That makes it easy to find number strings that roughly match short phrases. The match is usually approximate. Native speakers accept the fuzziness because context makes the meaning obvious.

May 20 (5/20) has become an unofficial “Internet Valentine’s Day”, with jewellery, flower and restaurant promotions. Some couples also register marriages on that date.

Everyday internet codes

  • 666 — “awesome / slick”, from 溜 (liù). Huge in gaming and livestream chat.
  • 233 — laughing (LOL), from a laughing-emoticon code on the old Mop.com forum.
  • 88 / 886 — bye-bye / bye then.
  • 555 — crying (呜呜呜).
  • 918 — 加油吧, “go for it!”
  • 9494 — 就是就是, “exactly!”

Codes to avoid

  • 250 — 二百五, “idiot”. Never price a gift at 250.
  • 748 — 去死吧, “go die”.
  • 7456 — 气死我了, “I’m furious”.
  • 38 — 三八, a sexist insult for a gossip.

FAQ

Is 666 negative in China?

No. It means “awesome”, the opposite of its Western association.

What does 1314 520 mean?

Together, “forever, I love you” — common in wedding and jewellery marketing.
