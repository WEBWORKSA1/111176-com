Why Chinese Buyers Value Numeric Domains

By the 111176 editorial team · Updated 2026-09-29 · 6 min read

Quick answer: For many Chinese users, digits are easier to remember and type than English words or ambiguous pinyin, and numbers carry lucky meanings. That demand drove the entire pool of one million 6-digit .com domains to be registered by November 2015.

Why numbers?

  • No spelling barrier: digits are the same on every keyboard. Pinyin names are ambiguous without tones.
  • Memorable: short numbers are easy to say, remember and put on a billboard.
  • Meaningful: 8 (prosper), 6 (smooth) and 9 (lasting) add perceived value.
  • Proven brands: 163.com (NetEase), 360.cn and 58.com are numeric household names.

Historic sales

Short numerics have commanded seven figures: 11.com reportedly sold for about $2.3M (2011) and 114.com for about $2.1M (2013), per Media Options. In 2015, demand swept through longer numerics. TheDomains reported that the last 6-digit .coms were being registered in early November 2015, with floor prices jumping sharply that autumn.

Reality check

Prices for long numerics fell after the 2015–2016 boom. By 2020, 7-digit .coms changed hands on Chinese platforms for very little (Domain Name Wire). Value now depends on pattern, length and actual end-user use. Always check recent comparable sales and run a trademark search before you buy or sell. Try our domain scorer or request an appraisal.

FAQ

How many 6N .com domains exist?

Exactly 1,000,000 (000000–999999). 262,144 of them contain no 0 and no 4.

Are numeric domains a good investment?

They can be liquid, but prices are cyclical. This is general information, not investment advice.
